# Go-live audit — 14 September 2026

## Phase 1: existing implementation, before edits

The authoritative settings record is `SiteSetting[commerce.pricing.v1]`, edited by `settings.manage` at Admin > Pricing. `commerce/engine.ts` computes protected contribution prices; `catalogue/retail-pricing.ts` wraps it for both distributors. No replacement engine is needed.

Let C be supplier cost excluding VAT, D delivery + surcharge + handling per unit, L=(C+D) × unrecoverable input VAT factor, V output VAT factor, p gateway percentage × unrecoverable fee VAT factor, f fixed fee including payout allocation, m minimum gateway fee, r reserve fraction, g cost-band contribution margin and M minimum rand contribution. Net revenue is the maximum of four branches: percentage-fee margin, percentage-fee minimum contribution, minimum-fee margin, minimum-fee minimum contribution. The percentage branch solves `(L+f)/(1-g-r-pV)` and `(L+f+M)/(1-r-pV)`. The minimum-fee branches replace percentage deductions with minimum gateway fee plus payout allocation. Gross is net × V, rounded upward. The public floor is the greater of PayFast and Ozow floors.

Existing defaults: 18/12/8% contribution margins; R120/350/750 minimum contribution across cost bands; supplier/input and fee VAT treatment depends on configured registration. These are configuration assumptions, not verified merchant contracts. Monthly platform expense is reporting-only. Local products keep manually published prices with a checkout floor guard. Supplier prices additionally used `max(engine floor, supplier RRP)` — contrary to the requested benchmark-only meaning. Promotional supplier costs already respect start/end dates. Coupons cannot consume protected floor headroom. Settings are included in immutable order-line snapshots.

Supplier feeds retain separate costs, RRP, promotion costs, availability and identities. Shared display reconciliation ranks eligible offers by unit cost; delivery allocation is presently global. Checkout recalculates server-side and compares a reviewed fingerprint. Gateway notifications verify signatures, reference, amount, currency and remote provider evidence. Row locks and unique gateway event IDs protect duplicate payment transitions. Customer confirmation occurs after commit, but is not durably queued inside the payment transaction. Staff and customer email use existing outbox/retry machinery. Procurement is manually recorded and does not call supplier ordering APIs.

Desktop orders require orders.view; mobile layout requires mobile-admin membership, but the orders page lacked orders.view. Customer order queries constrain userId. Browser return routes previously mutated failed/cancelled state based on unsigned URL parameters. Polling was unbounded. Customer tracker exposed supplier procurement stages and labelled dispatch/in-transit as out-for-delivery. All 78 baseline test files / 262 tests passed despite these gaps; many tests inspect source strings rather than exercising a database or browser.

## Phase 2: minimum design

Extend existing JSON pricing settings and existing engine with explicit optional overhead allocation and custom costs, benchmark-only supplier RRP, a guarded competitive adjustment, and an impact preview before publishing. Preserve existing cost-band minimums, tax treatment, upward rounding and order snapshots. Add no alternate engine and no automatic below-floor permission bypass.

Make browser returns read-only; bound polling; protect procurement with paid-state and commercial checks; strengthen mobile authorization; simplify public tracking using existing statuses. Surface unresolved operations using existing admin records. Validate with regression tests, lint, TypeScript and production build. Do not send live mail, place supplier orders, charge/refund money, or change secrets.

## Go-live report and verification boundary

This is not a production-ready declaration. The subsequent Pricing & Trading brief explicitly requires verification of the pricing approval journey before further platform implementation. No live pricing configuration, gateway transaction, supplier order, customer email or production secret was changed by this verification.

1. **Existing formula:** The four-branch calculation documented above solves percentage/fixed/minimum gateway costs, reserve, minimum contribution and contribution margin. Both gateways share the same configuration and engine.
2. **Refined formula:** Add configured economic custom costs and optional monthly cost divided by expected monthly units. Percentage-of-net custom costs enter the denominator. Compute both gateway floors, select the greater, apply competitive adjustment, enforce the floor again and round upward. Reject invalid/non-finite settings and non-viable denominators.
3. **Innozanzi RRP:** The system-calculated customer selling price under the active Innozanzi configuration. It is not the supplier's recommendation or a researched competitor price.
4. **Supplier RRP:** Retained as an optional internal benchmark; no longer forces selling price upward.
5. **Missing RRP:** Cost-based calculation remains valid for either supplier. The initial database audit found 1,711 active offers without RRP, with no calculation exceptions.
6. **Commercial floor:** Shared protected price calculation, checkout quotation validation, payment retry validation and paid-order commercial review. No below-floor bypass was added.
7. **Gateway fees:** Percentage, fixed, minimum, fee VAT and payout allocation are included. The public floor protects the more expensive configured gateway. Actual merchant contracts and the highest fee among enabled PayFast methods still require operator verification.
8. **Operating costs:** Monthly expense is reporting-only unless per-unit allocation is selected. Expected monthly units makes the divisor explicit. Custom components support per-item, conservatively per-unit protected per-order, monthly and percentage-of-net costs.
9. **Admin Pricing:** Simulator, optional RRP comparison, custom costs, full impact preview, confirmation and reason. The PostgreSQL advisory-lock result now casts to text, fixing the reproduced Prisma `void` deserialization failure. Publication atomically writes settings, active version and audit. Draft and draft audit save together; drafts can be reloaded. Expected errors are readable; unexpected failures have a trace reference. A post-commit cache refresh failure is distinguished from a rollback.
10. **Product pricing:** Both distributor wrappers use the same engine. Internal product economics show floor, allocations, fees, contribution and RRP difference. Local products retain their existing manual pricing. Preview explicitly discloses that local variants need separate review.
11. **Supplier integration:** Existing feeds and canonical identity are retained. Freshness, approval, purchasing and stock checks remain relevant. There is no supplier ordering API added. Supplier selection still ranks unit cost with a global delivery allocation; it cannot claim to optimise supplier-specific landed cost without actual delivery schedules.
12. **Checkout:** Server-side prices, discounts and total remain authoritative. Stable quotation/address/payment-method fingerprints avoid creating another payment for an identical repeated submission. Approval remains required. Commercial snapshots freeze order-time values; retries and the hosted payment page revalidate safety.
13. **Payment confirmation:** Browser return parameters cannot change payment state. Signed/provider-verified confirmation is required. Payment/order locks, gateway-event uniqueness and state checks protect duplicate and late callbacks. Reconciliation requires Ozow provider evidence or a signed, remotely validated PayFast notification. Ten older pending hosted payments found in the initial audit remain unresolved operational exceptions.
14. **Order lifecycle:** Paid-state and snapshot/current-cost checks protect procurement progression. Late captures on closed orders surface exceptions. Actual cost reconciliation records a separate audit result without overwriting order-time amounts. Supplier procurement remains an explicit operator workflow.
15. **Customer tracker:** Confirmed, processing, shipped and delivered map to actual existing events. No out-for-delivery event is invented from dispatch. A real carrier event/integration remains necessary to display that additional stage reliably.
16. **Email:** A durable paid-order communication job is created with the confirmed-payment transaction. The worker retries it. Invoice generation and outbox delivery use transaction/ownership/idempotency guards. The advisory-lock cast fix also applies to the outbox. Provider acceptance followed by a process crash before database commit can still duplicate an email because the current provider integration lacks a true provider-enforced idempotency key. Delivery is therefore not proven exactly-once; test this recovery path using test recipients before launch.
17. **Desktop Admin:** Expanded order lookup, commercial review, actual cost reconciliation and payment reconciliation evidence. Invoice creation is guarded against concurrent duplication. Customer invoice access follows order ownership.
18. **Mobile Admin:** Orders require `orders.view` as well as mobile access. Pending-payment and communication exceptions are surfaced. Time-based counts use the existing operational clock helper.
19. **Security:** Fixed unsigned return state mutation, missing mobile order permission, invoice ownership ambiguity and state transition weaknesses. Historical order values remain frozen. These targeted changes and tests are not a full penetration test or proof that every route is secure.
20. **Verification:** 85 test files / 289 tests passed. TypeScript passed. Production webpack build passed, including 139 static pages. Lint reported zero errors and four existing warnings. Latest read-only catalogue preview processed 4,441 offers in 2,690 ms with zero exceptions. The uncast advisory query reproduced the failure and the text-cast query succeeded on the configured database. Transactional rollback tests use a mocked transactional store; an authenticated browser approval-to-order journey has not been performed.
21. **Migrations:** This pricing-publication repair needs no schema migration; it uses existing SiteSetting and AuditLog models. Proposed trading models and SQL are stored under `docs/commerce/proposed-trading-*`, outside Prisma's deployable migrations. They are incomplete design material and must be reviewed before implementation or execution. No trading migration was applied.
22. **Configuration:** Use project-supported Node 22 (the verification environment currently runs Node 25). Confirm actual VAT registration, both merchant fee schedules, delivery/surcharge allocation, expected monthly units and custom costs. Verify the email automation worker and gateway notification URLs are deployed and monitored. Gateway credential presence in the audit does not prove live merchant activation or webhook delivery. No new secret is required for this pricing repair.
23. **Required manual acceptance:** Follow the checklist below before accepting real customer orders. The requested trading platform remains at design stage until the Phase 1 acceptance gate passes; no trading desk, live market scan or override engine is represented as complete.

### Operator acceptance checklist — remaining launch blockers

- Confirm contracted PayFast/Ozow percentage, fixed/minimum fees, fee VAT and enabled methods; confirm VAT registration and actual Syntech/Pinnacle delivery/surcharge assumptions. The audited database had no saved `commerce.pricing.v1`; defaults are not approved commercial settings.
- With an authorised admin, save the verified draft, reload it, preview the catalogue, enter the reason, confirm and publish. Record the active version. Refresh the page and verify that the version persists. A failed request must leave the previous version active and show an error reference.
- In a sandbox/test environment, test one Syntech product with RRP and one Pinnacle product without it. Verify product, cart and checkout prices/totals agree; create a test order and confirm the approval message is cleared after legitimate publication. Change supplier price during an unpaid checkout and verify the customer is asked to review it.
- Using gateway test mechanisms, exercise success, failed payment, return-before-webhook, delayed webhook, repeated webhook and a mismatched reference/amount. Expect one captured state transition, one active invoice and one fulfilment task. Do not use real customer money. Resolve the ten older hosted-payment attempts against provider evidence; age alone is not evidence of success or failure.
- Verify that the durable communication worker runs after a process restart, sends to test recipients and attaches an accessible invoice. Verify the crash/acceptance ambiguity with the configured mail provider. Missing confirmation or duplicate communication remains a launch risk until this is tested.
- Exercise desktop and mobile access with an operations account and an unauthorised account. Attempt another test customer's order and invoice URLs. Confirm supplier economics are not returned to customer-facing clients.
- Use test procurement and tracking records to verify commercial exceptions, supplier reference, dispatch, delivery and customer notifications. Do not place a real supplier order. Verify actual supplier-specific delivery costs before claiming lowest landed-cost source selection.
- Reconcile actual supplier/delivery/gateway costs on the test completed order and compare to the original estimate. Local variants, if published later, need their own pricing and discount safety checks.

Until these checks pass, unresolved risks include incorrect fee/delivery assumptions and margin, blocked checkout, gateway/order mismatch, missing or duplicate communication and inaccurate tracking. Building successfully does not resolve these risks.
